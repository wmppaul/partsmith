# Broader native paired-ending review — experimental

The ending-only native run finished successfully on 14 additional fixed-layout scores (381 physical pages), using immutable source PDFs, hash-bound staff inventories, original profiles and a frozen copy of current Core. All 36 original corpus inputs (1,477 pages) are recorded in `roster.json`: 19 variable-layout profiles disable this path, one fixed-layout input remains unresolved, and the two previous positives were verified separately rather than scanned again. Those skipped inputs are not negative results. All physical pages remain in pairing order with sparse/error barriers. No production code, source pixels, detector geometry or frozen guard was changed.

Source review found six genuine selected pairs, with no selected-pair false positives. All 12 members and seven exact copied source rows retain their numerals, lines and hooks. Some copies retain incidental neighboring ink, including beam/staff edges, accents and note/slur fragments. The source review of all 65 numeral candidates classified 12 selected genuine members, seven unpaired genuine first endings, and 46 rejected notation fragments. Seven visible paired endings were missed: both Brahms 242312 and 09200 on physical pages 21/system 3, 23/system 4 and 24/system 2; Schumann 06822 on page 29/system 3. These are observed misses, not a complete whole-score recall inventory.

All 5,315 original main crops and identities/order in the 14-score scan remain exact. The three newly positive scores were fully exported from current Core with and without endings: 13 parts in each version, with 157 candidate pages versus 155 control/prior pages, with original profiles and prior title/composer metadata. The 1,785 exported main crops remain exact against both the same-Core controls and earlier corpus exports. Adding ending rows increases Schumann Cello from 11 to 12 pages and Piano from 26 to 27 pages; other part counts are unchanged. Every before/after output page was rendered; `export-comparison.json` records every placement, raster hash, source geometry, overflow and collision check. The 26 new recipient rows were viewed with their own staves after source review. Placement changes are expected consequences of adding vertical space and page balancing, and the independent output review is tracked separately.

**Known output defect:** all five added Schumann Piano rows duplicate complete endings already printed above that part's own staff: p6 systems 1 and 2, p16 system 3, p18 system 2, and p20 system 3 (output pages 3, 8, 9 and 10). Preserve these exports as experimental evidence, not publication-ready delivery. The Cello p18 system 2 crop also retains the neighboring Piano pair at its lower edge; that is not a Cello-owned counterpart. A separate scratch experiment will require a complete local pair at the same system/bar position and wholly retained by the recipient crop before suppressing any automatic copy.

`candidate-source-review.json`, `recipient-row-review.json` and `review-summary.json` give the explicit visual judgments. `selected-pairs.json` records selected native metadata; `exact-copy-images.json` binds source copies. Source images, output-row images, input/binary/export hashes and execution checkpoints are retained. This review establishes selected-copy preservation and detects concrete defects; it does not establish whole-score musical completeness, staff identity correctness on every corpus page, clean crops, or broad ending recall.

The native runner finished with exit 0 and no OCR/render errors. Scratch outputs and all rendered pages are under `.build/ending-corpus-2026-10-03/`. Output PDFs and embedded projects were not promoted or overwritten.
